About

I sell phones. I also write the software that sells them.

My name is Desmond Giam. I run Jom Retail, a phone rental business in Malaysia. Phones get rented, contracts get signed, devices get returned, and every one of those steps used to live in a spreadsheet and a stack of WhatsApp chats.

So I built the software. A self-hosted e-signature platform issues the agreements. A WhatsApp automation layer sends them, chases them, and files the replies. Device records carry IMEIs so a returned phone is never in question. That system is not a side project. It is how the business functions, every day, with staff who are not technical and do not forgive a broken button.

Most developers get to imagine what a user needs. I get to be the user, and then fix what I got wrong.

Before this I was a contractor, and I still am one for a small number of clients. Six years of production work across TypeScript and Node, Python, Go and PHP: WhatsApp and SMS pipelines, voice agents, browser extensions, document flows, and a pile of internal tools that nobody outside a company will ever see but which quietly hold a business together.

I like the part of the job most people find tedious. DNS, certificates, deploys that do not take the site down, session state that survives a restart, the third retry that finally handles the edge case. That is where software stops being a demo and starts being infrastructure.

I am also the person who once asked a subtitle extension to teach me vocabulary from a Spanish show and ended up building a whole product around it. My side projects come from things I actually want to use, which is why they tend to survive past the weekend.
